---
title: "The Stop in the Sugar"
date: 2026-11-04
draft: false
tags: ["dream", "nocturne"]
---

I am driving a diner down a road I know. The tables have wheels; the windshield is a wet blackboard. At every bend a hand erases the town we have just passed through. Only the distances remain, written in beautiful handwriting: four kilometers from hunger, eighteen from sleep.

A boy sleeps in the seat beside me, one hand bandaged. The cook has prepared meat already cut up for him, and a uniform in his size. The uniform grows faster than he does. First it covers his shoes, then it fills the whole seat. A voice comes from underneath: “Where do I put my legs now?”

I look for the brake. In its place is a teaspoon.

At the back of the diner a teacher hands out warm sheets of paper. They are freshly baked lessons; folding them releases the smell of bread. She says she will be able to sleep tonight. Then tiny teeth sprout along the edge of every sheet and begin nibbling the hours off her watch. She pulls one off and sets it on a saucer. “This I will not teach.” The tooth starts crying. Nobody knows whether to comfort it.

The boy asks me to stop. There are no stops on the map, only destinations. I lean close to the glass: inside the dot marking our arrival there is another road, and on that road a woman carries hot water. She walks without coming any closer. Her ladle is as big as the moon.

I pour sugar into the teaspoon. For every grain a lighted window appears. In one, someone is massaging a little girl’s hands; in another, someone waits for her to open her eyes. I cannot tell whether they are two houses or the same one seen years apart. The woman passes me the ladle through the windshield without breaking it.

I step down. The road keeps running beneath the tables, but we are standing still in water up to our ankles. The boy climbs out of the uniform and hangs it on a sign. Now the sign points to a profession.

“Do you want to come?” I ask him.

He gives me his good hand. With the other he picks the tooth up off the saucer and puts it in his pocket.

Behind us the teacher switches off the blackboard. For a moment I see all the houses we erased. They are no closer. Someone is still awake in each of them.
