title: "The Instrument Store"

There is a cloakroom beneath the sea. To enter, I have to hang my body on a hanger; I can keep the hat. The woman at the counter gives me two identical tickets and says one is for coming back, the other for staying. Both are wet.

In the main hall an orchestra is rehearsing without musicians. Their jackets sit on the chairs, swollen with breath. The piano has small, crooked teeth. Somewhere I recognize a phrase I have never known how to play; as soon as I approach, it changes key and becomes the sound of the pipes at home.

A child drags a blackboard. He has to get it to school before the night ends. He has drawn two overlapping circles, and a dog sleeps in the part they share. The adults argue over which circle the animal belongs to. The child erases the outlines so as not to wake it. Now there is only the dog on the blackboard, but nobody knows where to push it anymore.

The conductor asks me to keep time. He gives me a silver triangle with no beater. I hold it between my teeth, warm it in my palm, bring it to my ear. Nothing. The jackets keep playing and all I hear is my blood. Then a sleeve stretches out and takes my elbow: not to guide me, to ask me to move. I was blocking the light for a woman sewing.

She sews pockets inside pockets. In each she puts a different pause. The longest are for people who need to catch their breath on the stairs. She asks which one I want to take away. I choose the one where the orchestra makes a mistake together, stops, and laughs. It will not fit in my coat: it keeps catching on the other voices.

At the cloakroom my body has grown older. It has hanger marks on its shoulders and holds an empty cup. The woman compares the tickets, raises them to the lamp. Through the paper I see the dog walking from one circle to the other without leaving its sleep.

“One instrument is missing,” she says.

I go back to the hall. The jackets have collapsed, the piano is closed. Under a chair I find my breath. It is small and resists when I try to slip it into my pocket. I let it climb in on its own.

I wake on the stairs. Someone on the floor above is still laughing.
