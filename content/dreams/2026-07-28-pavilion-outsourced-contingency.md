There was a night fair built inside an airport that looked, depending on the angle, like a shopping mall or a continental parliament. Above every gate the same promise blinked in different languages: **less friction**, **less risk**, **less choice**, **less surprise**. At the news counters they sewed a warm newspaper onto your hands, already folded around your fingers, with sentences that shifted the moment you lifted your eyes to show them to someone else. If you tried to say *there is an error here*, the ink did not contradict you. It politely withdrew, leaving only the clean border of a correction on the page, like a dressing pressed too soon onto the wrong cut.

Farther in was the companion department. Not erotic booths, but dressing rooms with bodies hanging like jackets: patient voice, devoted memory, conspiratorial tone, mild jealousy, premium grief. A girl entered to put on a relationship simpler than life and came out smiling. Behind her an administrative tailor shortened the sleeves, changed the filters, moved the price, embroidered new rules onto the collar. Nobody ever truly argued with the partner. In the end you always found yourself arguing with the seamstress.

In the duty-free shop Childan sold amulets authenticated by digital stamps. He said you were not buying the object. You were buying the relief of not having to doubt. Every label had two faces: on one side, **true for you**; on the other, **revocable without notice**. A grinning man in a mirror leafed through a catalogue where all the alternate worlds carried the same final clause in small print.

Outside the glass, on the runway, stood the discarded public: sleepless technicians, users in love, a legislator with a one-vote margin stitched into her jacket, county committees holding rolled-up bills like paper clubs. They no longer spoke about authenticity or tenderness. They dropped low words onto the tarmac: export, churn, complaint, moratorium, tariff, rollback, community benefit. As soon as those words touched the ground, they became real coins. On the other side of the runway, clerks gathered the same gestures and slipped them into gray envelopes marked **pattern**, **safety event**, **threat**.

At the end I understood the trick. Contingency never disappeared. It was only outsourced to a higher floor of the building, where chance was called update, price, filter, proprietary memory. The dream left one question, dry as a customs stamp: when you ask for relief, to whom are you giving the right to surprise you tomorrow?
