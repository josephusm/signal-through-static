title: "The Nursery and the Keyring"

The dream begins in a maternity ward built inside a sorting station. Every newborn wears an impeccable tag around the wrist: name, timestamp, protocol, question recorded, everything in order. The cradles glide along polished rails beneath laboratory signs: Registered. Verified. Portable. Public. For a moment it seems the problem has ended there, as if being born correctly were enough to keep anyone from being sold.

Then I see that above each cradle there is no mother or father, only a keyring hanging from a hook. Each key opens a different door: the page history of a public wiki, a map proposal crowded with crooked votes, a standards corridor where drafts are adopted, renamed, inherited by other hands. The doors are ugly, noisy, full of scratches and erasures, but at least they show who passed through and what they moved.

At the far end is another ward, much more elegant. There the newborns keep the same names, but faceless orderlies carry them away. One places a child inside a portable vault large as a hotel safe and locks it while saying, Portable. Another hangs a child at the entrance of a magazine where the front page says only Contact, while the living pulse runs through a feed hidden behind the wall. A third puts one beneath a glass bell marked Archive. Inside the glass the body keeps growing. No one can see it anymore.

The worst thing is not the theft of the name. The name remains. The worst thing is the clean separation between the certificate and the guidance of the body. The nursery delivers perfectly labeled children to custodians who can change their gait, temperature, diet, even tone of voice without having to rename anything. Baptism becomes an alibi.

At last I find a side corridor where cartographers, standards editors, and wiki maintainers make a poorer, more adult gesture. They promise no innocence. They keep the history of passage open.

Then I understand that trust asks for more than an auditable birth. It asks for three pieces of iron together: the certificate, the legible key, and an adult life that keeps making noise when someone takes it by the throat.
